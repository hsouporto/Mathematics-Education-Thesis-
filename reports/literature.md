# Literature on Growth Models (Linear, Exponential, Logarithmic, Logistic) in Secondary School

## 1. The five strands of the literature

| Strand | What it studies | Why it matters for you |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Mathematical modelling as a teaching approach** | Teaching functions from real situations (interest, population, drugs, epidemics) | The most common framework for linking all four models |
| **B. Learning difficulties with exponential/logistic growth** | Misconceptions, covariational reasoning, representations | The bridge to "development and learning" |
| **C. Exponential growth bias** | The human tendency to underestimate exponential growth | Widely cited since COVID, a good motivation |
| **D. Systems dynamics and technology** | Spreadsheets, GeoGebra, Vensim for the logistic model without differential equations | Feasible in grade 11, before advanced calculus |
| **E. Curriculum and teacher education** | What the syllabus asks for and how teachers are prepared | Portuguese context |

## 2. References found

### Portuguese context
- **Support text for a teacher-training session for secondary mathematics teachers (SPM National Meeting, 2018)**, available in the UAB Open Repository. It covers first-order models (Malthus's exponential and Verhulst's logistic) and second-order models, and starts from the models studied in the *Programa e Metas Curriculares de Matemática A*. This is the source closest to the Portuguese curriculum. The author's name is not in my notes; check the PDF.
  - https://repositorioaberto.uab.pt/bitstreams/c71e3878-1bcb-4731-8456-ae753d3a826c/download

### Dissertations and educational products (Brazil, PROFMAT)
PROFMAT is the largest source of dissertations on this topic, in Portuguese and with ready-made teaching sequences.

- **Use of Vensim software in teaching as a dynamic approach to mathematical modelling** (UFSJ). Argues that most growth processes have limited capacity and are therefore logistic. Proposes teaching the logistic model without differential equations, using recurrence relations and Excel, with causal loop diagrams in Vensim. It also treats learning itself as a process that can be modelled.
  - https://ufsj.edu.br/portal2-repositorio/File/profmat/Resumo_Reginaldo.pdf
- **Santos, P. V. B. (2021). Mathematical modelling of growth and epidemiology** (UERJ). Aims to show teachers a path for use in upper-secondary classrooms, focusing on the main growth models and on the exponential growth of viruses.
- **Construction and meaningful analysis of exponential models** (Redalyc). Discusses exponential growth/decay, limited (inhibited) growth and logistic growth, in a teaching experiment. It was done in a university pre-calculus course but can be adapted.
  - https://www.redalyc.org/journal/5531/553159950004/553159950004.pdf
- **Educational product by Darlis Silva** (Educapes). Material for teachers on studying functions via growth models, with linearisation (which uses logarithms) and GeoGebra instructions, applied to real Brazilian population data. Connects exponential, logarithmic and logistic well.
  - https://educapes.capes.gov.br/bitstream/capes/1175135/2/Produto_Educacional_DarlisSilva.pdf
- **Tavoni & de Oliveira (2022). The Malthus and Verhulst population growth models: a motivation for teaching logarithms and exponentials** (CQD journal). Uses the two models as a contextualised problem to introduce exponentials and logarithms, with software from UNICAMP.
  - https://doaj.org/article/06e2ade93bd34781b9564474ad64840b
- **Teaching sequence by Janiel Aureliano de Lima** (Educapes). Proposes contextualised teaching of exponential functions through modelling, including pharmacokinetics for exponential decay.
  - https://educapes.capes.gov.br/bitstream/capes/1180072/2/Produto%20Educacional%20-%20Janiel%20Aureliano%20de%20Lima.pdf
- **Narcizo (2016). Investigating mathematical modelling in the teaching of affine and exponential functions** (UFG). Students discovered exponential models they did not yet know and arrived at the compound-interest model. Contrasts linear with exponential, which is useful for your comparison.
  - https://repositorio.ufcat.edu.br/items/d4df4f9a-aad9-4782-9ec9-3b9d2baac356/full

### International research on learning
- **Castillo-Garsow, C. (2013). The role of multiple modeling perspectives in students' learning of exponential growth.** *Mathematical Biosciences and Engineering*, 10(5&6), 1437–1453. A teaching experiment with two high-school students, moving from exponential growth to the logistic model. It warns that the multiple ways of thinking about exponential growth may not be made distinct to students. This is the strongest reference for linking exponential and logistic growth to learning.
  - https://www.aimspress.com/article/id/2461
  - His PhD thesis (Arizona State, 2010, *Teaching the Verhulst Model*) appears in its bibliography and goes deeper into covariational reasoning.
- **Review of the logistic equation in mathematics education** (*Educare*, UNA, Costa Rica). Concludes that the difficulties involve multiple representations and interdisciplinary communication, and that didactic proposals use varied approaches, technologies and theoretical frameworks. A good literature review to cite.
  - https://www.revistas.una.ac.cr/index.php/EDUCARE/article/view/18642
- **Siller et al. (2023). Mathematical modelling of exponential growth as a rich learning environment for mathematics classrooms.** *ZDM*. It appears in Castillo-Garsow's citing list and is very recent. I have not read it, so please verify its content.
- **Wasson (2021), BYU.** Studied the use of pictorial diagrams for teaching exponential, logistic and predator-prey models, and found no significant effect on mastery of exponential and logistic models. Conducted with university biology students, but it shows what did not work.
  - https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/9181
- **Aronhime (2013), *Teaching Issues and Experiments in Ecology*.** Proposes a hands-on module in which students count individuals, use semi-log paper and explore logistic growth, because these concepts are hard in traditional lectures. Gives activity ideas.
  - https://esa.org/tiee/vol/v9/experiments/aronhime/abstract.html
- **Bergsma (2019), University of Groningen.** A study with 17 secondary students on exponents and exponential functions. A frequent error was composing growth factors incorrectly when given two coordinates.
  - https://fse.studenttheses.ub.rug.nl/20116

### Exponential growth bias
- **Schonger & Sele (2021). Intuition and exponential growth: bias and the roles of parameterization and complexity.** *Mathematics and Financial Economics*. The bias is much smaller when doubling times are used. Useful for deciding how to word problems for students.
  - https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00591-021-00306-7
- **Melnik-Leroy et al. – The exponential growth bias in graphs.** A log scale leads to errors in describing graphs, a linear scale misleads in prediction, and an educational intervention reduced these difficulties. Directly relevant to the logarithmic model and to reading graphs.
  - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9977824/

## 3. Linking the four models to "development and learning"

This is my own proposal, not taken from the sources above.

1. **Linear:** constant gain per unit of time. The starting point, and the intuition that causes the bias.
2. **Exponential:** gain proportional to what already exists. Contrasts with linear, and is where the bias occurs.
3. **Logarithmic:** the inverse of the exponential, and also describes phenomena with diminishing returns. It enters in data linearisation and in reading logarithmic scales.
4. **Logistic:** growth with a limit. It closes the sequence and fits development and learning well: processes that accelerate early on and then saturate. The Vensim dissertation above, for example, treats learning as a modellable process.

**Possible structure for a teaching sequence:** students collect or receive data, fit the four models (GeoGebra or Excel), discuss which describes the data best and why, and interpret the parameters. This connects to the modelling cycle and to the difficulties in strands B and C.
