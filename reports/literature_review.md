# Literature on Growth Models in Secondary Mathematics

Linear, exponential, logarithmic and logistic models in Grade 11, with a focus on learning and development.

> **How to use this list:** every URL below was returned by a search and the annotations come from the abstracts or catalogue pages. I have not read the full texts. Check each source before citing it. Items marked ⚠ need extra checking (year, author or access).

## 1. How students learn exponential growth (core cognitive literature)

| Reference | What it offers | Link |
|---|---|---|
| Confrey, J., & Smith, E. (1995). Splitting, covariation, and their role in the development of exponential functions. *Journal for Research in Mathematics Education*, 26(1), 66–86. | Foundational paper. Argues for building the exponential function from "splitting" (a multiplicative operation that is not repeated addition) and a covariation approach to functions. | Abstract: https://mathed.net/wiki/Confrey_%26_Smith_(1995) · DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/749228 |
| Castillo-Garsow, C. (2013). The role of multiple modeling perspectives in students' learning of exponential growth. *Mathematical Biosciences and Engineering*, 10(5&6), 1437–1453. | Teaching experiment with two high-school students, from exponential growth to the logistic differential equation model. Warns that the multiple ways of thinking about exponential growth are often not made distinct for students. | https://www.aimspress.com/article/id/2461 · PDF: https://www.aimspress.com/fileOther/PDF/MBE/1551-0018_2013_5-6_1437.pdf |
| Ellis, A. B., Ozgur, Z., Kulow, T., Dogan, M. F., Williams, C. C., & Amidon, J. (2013). Correspondence and covariation: Quantities changing together. *Proceedings of PME-NA 35*, 119–126. | Small teaching experiment on exponential functions with two continuously covarying quantities (height and time). ## 5. Suggested reading order for a thesis

1. **Framing:** Confrey & Smith (1995), then Castillo-Garsow (2013).
2. **Difficulties and misconceptions:** Educare review (logistic), Bergsma (2019), Ellis et al. (2013).
3. **Intuition and graphs:** Schonger & Sele (2021), Melnik-Leroy et al. (2023).
4. **Modelling approach:** Siller et al. (2023), the SPM 2018 text for the Portuguese syllabus.
5. **Practical sequences:** the PROFMAT products (Darlis Silva, Lima, Tavoni & Oliveira).
